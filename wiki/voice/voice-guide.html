Decision (2026-09-03): the go-forward voice is the Digital Landlord register. The calm-authority register belongs to the outgoing website but its best habits carry over. The four published articles sit between the two and are the best evidence of how Daniele actually writes long-form.

Go-forward voice: The Digital Landlord

  • Direct, second person, contrarian, concrete. Hook first, then plain explanation.
  • Metaphors: rent vs own, landlord, property, storefront, website as employee. See digital real estate.
  • Contrast structure: "Stop X, start Y"; "not a brochure, your best salesperson".
  • Audience call-outs: "To the business owner who...", "For the overthinkers who...".
  • Keep from the old register: no jargon, no hype, honest about price, says who it is not for.
  • Do not publish numeric claims without proof (see the caution list in content hooks).

How Daniele writes long-form (published articles, 2026)

  • Opens by naming the reader's situation in one or two short lines: "Nobody's enquiring. Nobody's buying."
  • Reframes early and bluntly: the problem is "almost certainly not" the design; "fine... is the same as invisible".
  • Uses numbered lists of causes and price bands; gives real numbers (£ bands, 1 to 3 percent conversion, 50 milliseconds).
  • Local anchoring: Stratford, Hackney, Bethnal Green, "I see this constantly in East London".
  • Coins or borrows a label for the problem: "AI Regret", "the strategy gap", "expensive digital brochure".
  • Short punch sentences after a longer set-up: "But they're 10x more important."
  • Occasional generic AI-flavoured paragraph (the "lavish party" analogy in the conversion article) that reads off-voice; avoid.

Outgoing register: calm authority (website, owner replies, GBP post)

  • Tone: measured, reassuring, adult-to-adult. Sells clarity and relief, not excitement. Signature tagline: "Calm Authority, Broader Reach".
  • Stance: consultant, steward, risk-reducer. Never the hype merchant. Repeatedly disclaims pressure: "No pressure. No pitch. Just a grounded, strategic conversation."
  • Structure: name the discomfort, reframe it ("This isn't a design issue. It's a business clarity and stewardship issue."), then offer a calmer path. Frequent triads: "clarity, credibility, and confidence"; "strategy, stewardship, and accountability".
  • Contrast pairs: "asset, not decoration"; "infrastructure, not a trend-driven project"; "steward" vs "react"; "build structure" vs "chase trends"; "fewer decisions, not more tools".
  • Qualification is part of the voice. Every page says who it is not for: cheapest option, hacks, trends, early-stage experiments. "That clarity protects everyone's time."
  • Vocabulary: stewardship, credibility, clarity, calm, dependable, grounded, fragile/fragility, misalignment, accountability, business-first, commercial performance, quietly (undermining, costing, working in the background).
  • Owner replies on Google: short, warm, informal, with the odd emoji (😎 🙌). "Thanks so much for the kind words, really appreciate it." Occasional typos left in ("Wa a pleasure"). One reply uses "we" and a more corporate tone; the rest are first person.
  • GBP post voice: practical listicle, numbered emoji, one concrete stat, ends on the benefit ("significantly increase enquiries").

Banned or avoided (inferred from the site's own disclaimers)

hype, jargon, "hacks", "tricks", "keyword stuffing", upselling language, urgency pressure, promising rankings, "cheapest".
