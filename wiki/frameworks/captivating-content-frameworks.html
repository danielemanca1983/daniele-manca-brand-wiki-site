Four teaching frameworks chosen by Daniele on 2026-09-16, one for each content pillar. They sit under the organising metaphor of digital real estate. Confidence is low because each is a freshly chosen draft, not yet tested in published content or on a call. Each entry carries a ready-to-paste image prompt so a visual can be produced in any image model; drop in the brand colours.

1. Property first, then tenants (pillar: Own, don't rent)

Type: two-step formula. The idea: build the property, then, and only then, pay for traffic. Ads amplify what is there; on a weak site they amplify the leak. This is also Daniele's actual sales rule: ads are an optional add-on "only when the site is ready to convert" (see The Digital Landlord).

Image prompt:

A clean, editorial, minimal two-step illustration on a plain background with plenty of negative space. Left side, labelled "1. Property": a simple line-drawn small shopfront or house, solid foundations visible beneath it, with three small labels attached: "clear message", "obvious next step", "proof". Right side, labelled "2. Tenants": the same building, now with a stream of small figures walking toward its open door, and above them a small tag reading "ads, only now". Between the two steps, a single bold arrow pointing left to right. Below the whole image, one line of caption text: "Property first. Then tenants." Flat vector style, thin lines, one accent colour on the arrow and the door (use your brand colour), no gradients, no clutter, no extra text.

2. The 11pm Test (pillar: A site that works)

Type: three-question formula. The idea: a stranger on their phone at 11pm, who has never heard of you: can they work out what you do, trust you, and contact you, in under a minute, without help? Three yes-or-no questions. Fail any one and there is a leak. An evolution of the 7 Mar 2026 GBP post on three website mistakes.

Image prompt:

A clean, editorial, minimal illustration on a plain background with plenty of negative space. Centre: a single smartphone drawn in thin lines, screen glowing softly, with a small clock icon in the corner reading "11pm". To the right of the phone, three short lines stacked vertically, each with an empty checkbox beside it: "Do I know what you do?", "Do I trust you?", "Can I contact you now?". Below the phone, a small stopwatch icon with the text "under 60 seconds". At the bottom, one line of caption: "The 11pm Test. Fail one, you have a leak." Flat vector style, dark background or light background to match your brand, one accent colour on the phone screen and the checkboxes, no people, no clutter, no extra text.

3. The 18-Month Cliff (pillar: The real cost of cheap)

Type: timeline. The idea: two timelines. Cheap site: launch, a year of patches and workarounds, rebuild at month 18, back to zero. Built to work: build once, then it keeps working and compounding. The shaded gap between the lines is the real cost of cheap. Grounded in the cost guide's own claim that template and AI-builder sites cost £200 to £500 a year in fixes and are often rebuilt within 18 months (see pricing bands).

Image prompt:

A clean, editorial, minimal timeline chart on a plain background with plenty of negative space. Two horizontal timelines stacked, both running left to right from "Month 0" to "Month 24". Top line labelled "Cheap site": a small flag at Month 0 reading "launch", a cluster of small warning marks between Months 3 and 15 labelled "patches and workarounds", then the line drops off a sharp cliff edge at Month 18 with a label "rebuild", and starts again from zero. Bottom line labelled "Built to work": a small flag at Month 0 reading "build", then a smooth line that rises gently and steadily all the way to Month 24, with a small label near the end reading "still working". A shaded band between the two lines labelled "the real cost of cheap". Thin lines, flat vector style, one accent colour for the rising line and a muted grey for the cliff line (use your brand colours), no gradients, no clutter, no extra text.

4. Reputation vs Record (pillar: Found where they're looking)

Type: side-by-side. The idea: left, reputation: what customers know, word of mouth, years of good work. Right, record: what Google and AI can actually read (the Profile, the pages, the reviews). The gap between them is every search being missed. Property management is the work of closing the gap, which makes the monthly plan concrete.

Image prompt:

A clean, editorial, minimal side-by-side illustration on a plain background with plenty of negative space. Left panel titled "Reputation": a small line-drawn shopfront with a few smiling customers outside, a handshake icon, and a speech bubble reading "everyone round here knows them". Right panel titled "Record": the same shopfront, but drawn faintly, half-transparent, with a magnifying glass icon and a search bar above it showing an empty result, and a small text tag reading "what Google and AI can read". Between the two panels, a clearly marked gap with a double-headed arrow and the label "every search you're missing". At the bottom, one caption line: "Being good is not the same as being found." Flat vector style, thin lines, one accent colour on the left panel only so the right panel reads as faded (use your brand colours), no gradients, no clutter, no extra text.
