Who

Daniele Manca is a web designer, developer, and digital strategist running a solo agency from Leyton, East London (E10). Eighteen years in the industry, twelve-plus in web development, with an earlier background in online ad management. He works with small and medium sized businesses across London in any niche, and clients often find him on Google and value that he is local and will meet in person. Google rating 5.0 from 10 reviews. Website danielemanca.co.uk (being replaced).

Positioning: The Digital Landlord

Confirmed positioning and offer name. Draft statement: for local business owners who are tired of being invisible, Daniele turns your website into a 24/7 sales team so you can stop renting traffic from Google and start owning your local market.

Core metaphor, digital real estate: paying for ads and boosted posts is renting customers. A website plus local search presence is property that keeps producing without rent. Daniele is the landlord or property manager who builds and maintains the asset so the owner does not have to. SEO is "digital property management", not a tech headache. The website is your most valuable employee, a 24/7 sales team, not a brochure or a "digital paperweight".

Pillars: own don't rent; the website as best employee; local and in person; design and development in one person; jargon-free and education-first; AI-ready presence (visible in AI search summaries as well as Google Maps).

The previous positioning, "The Website-as-an-Asset System" with the tagline "Calm Authority, Broader Reach", is outgoing. Its useful habits carry over: no hype, no jargon, honest pricing, saying who the service is not for, and a free strategy call as the entry point.

Ideal client

Owner-run SMBs in London with a physical location or local service area. Any niche. Real client mix so far: electricians and trades, small Shopify and WooCommerce shops, first-website owners, professional service and B2B firms.

Current state: overwhelmed by digital noise, juggling manual operations, a website that looks dated or does not convert, invisible in local search, sceptical and burnt out from "throwing money" at ads with no clear return. Desired state: a "set it and forget it" engine, waking up to qualified inquiries, a professional presence that builds instant trust, a clear line from spend to revenue.

Core problem: the invisible business. A real business that does not appear when local customers search on Google or AI tools, losing share daily to competitors in the Local Pack.

Pains, ranked: outdated website destroying trust (critical), wasted ad spend (critical), invisible in local search (moderate), stuck e-commerce growth (moderate), technical overwhelm (low). Confirmed by real reviews: burned by previous designers ("years of hopping from one web designer to another with failed attempts"), doubt that a fair price can buy good work, wanting a local person to meet.

What they have tried: Wix or Squarespace sites that look amateur, cheap offshore SEO, boosted Facebook posts, Google Ads with no calls. Latent objections: SEO and lead-gen scepticism, fear of lock-in contracts, fear of paying for what cannot be measured.

Offers and pricing

The Digital Landlord, an offer under the Daniele Manca brand. Four parts: the storefront (a trust-building, converting website on WordPress or Shopify), the location (Google Business Profile and local search foundations, including readiness for AI search), the property manager (optional plan from £50 a month: weekly check-ups and plugin updates), and ads as an optional add-on from £500 plus media spend.

Three tiers priced "from". Simple, from £750: a clean, fast, trust-building site with local search foundations. Advanced, from £1,500: 10+ pages, an AI concierge on the site that answers visitors and qualifies leads, and a blog with three tailor-written posts. Pro, price to be set: everything in Advanced plus a tailored FAQ page, location and project pages built for the niche, and custom functionality such as a quotation tool. Optional property management from £50 a month for weekly check-ups and plugin updates, not included in any tier. Timeline from four weeks. No lock-in: the site, domain, and Google profile belong to the client. One person designs and builds.

Entry point: a free 30-minute video strategy consultation. "No pressure. No pitch." The call reviews the current site and Google presence, identifies what is holding the business back, and scopes the right tier. Fallbacks: contact form and WhatsApp.

Note: Daniele's published cost guide places substance at £3,000 to £5,000 and warns against sub-£800 sites. That article predates the new tiers and will be revised; do not quote it against the offer.

Voice

Direct, second person, contrarian, concrete. Hook first, then a plain explanation. Contrast structure: "Stop X, start Y"; "not a brochure, your best salesperson". Audience call-outs: "To the business owner who...". Real numbers and local anchors (Stratford, Hackney, Bethnal Green, "I see this constantly in East London"). Names the problem with a label ("AI Regret", "the strategy gap"). Short punch sentences after a set-up. Warm and informal in replies, the odd emoji.

Keep: no jargon, no hype, honest about price, says who it is not for. Avoid: hacks, tricks, keyword stuffing, guaranteed rankings, urgency pressure, "cheapest", generic analogies that read as AI filler.

Sample hooks in the confirmed voice: "Stop paying Google rent for traffic you should own." "Is your website a 24/7 sales team or just a digital paperweight?" "SEO isn't a tech headache, it's just digital property management." "Your website isn't a brochure, it's your most valuable employee." "You built a great business, now it's time to build a great digital home." "Finally, a website that works as hard as you do."

Market

Competitors: Thryv (all-in-one software, expensive, hard-to-exit contracts, poor support), generic local SEO agencies (opaque, cookie-cutter, spammy backlinks), and DIY builders plus ads as substitutes. Gaps: personalised high-touch service for local businesses ignored by big agencies, and education-first marketing that explains the why. Where the audience gathers: r/smallbusiness, r/localmarketing, local Chamber of Commerce Facebook groups.

Content pillars: website strategy and clarity; credibility and buyer confidence; conversion and commercial performance; SEO, visibility and AI discovery; ads, scale and risk. Published: the East London website cost guide, why your website isn't converting, why your AI-generated website leaks leads, why most business websites fail.
